  <h1>My First Semester Journey</h1>
  <p>From a new city to a new life — everything I went through in my first semester</p>
</div>

<div class="intro-section">

<p>Reaching UET Faisalabad was not a simple or straightforward path for me. During my 12th-grade exams, I had to undergo an appendix operation, which badly affected both my health and my studies, and I ended up losing a full academic year. It was a hard period — recovering physically while also feeling like I was falling behind everyone around me. But I didn't let it stop me. I cleared my supply exams, restarted my preparation for the ECAT, and even though I knew my prep wasn't as strong as I wanted, I gave it everything I had. Alhamdulillah, I passed, and got admission to UET Faisalabad — a moment that felt like the reward for not giving up.</p>

<p>Moving from Lahore to Faisalabad and into a hostel for the first time was a completely new kind of challenge. My first night there is still vivid in my memory — the room felt unfamiliar, the bed wasn't my own, and the silence around me was nothing like home. Nobody in that hostel knew anyone else; we were all strangers thrown into the same building, each of us quietly nervous in our own way. I missed my family more than I expected to, and for the first time in my life I understood what it actually means to be on your own. I didn't sleep much that night, but somehow I made it through, and looking back, that single night taught me more about independence than any class ever could.</p>

<p>The next morning was my first real day as a university student. I woke up early, got ready, and walked to my department with a group of equally nervous classmates. We searched for our classroom together, sat through our first introduction session, and slowly the awkwardness turned into comfort. A few seniors stopped us near the cafeteria for a light "introduction round," which was intimidating at first but turned out to be harmless. Later that day, a handful of us who had just become friends explored the campus together — the library, the playgrounds, the mosque — and by the time we returned to the hostel that night, exhausted but smiling, it finally felt like university life had actually begun.</p>

<p>What I didn't expect was how much of that first semester would have nothing to do with books at all. Almost every new student goes through the same invisible struggle — learning to manage your own time without a parent reminding you, learning to budget your pocket money so it lasts the month, learning to do your own laundry and figure out hostel mess timings, and learning to balance homesickness with the excitement of a new life. Some weeks felt lonely, especially in the beginning, and some weeks felt like everything was happening too fast. I think every fresh student goes through this same emotional rollercoaster — it's rarely talked about, but it's just as much a part of the first semester as any subject on the timetable.</p>

<p>Academically, the semester introduced me to a completely new set of subjects — Programming Fundamentals, Calculus, Discrete Mathematics, AICT, Applied Physics, and Fahm-e-Quran, along with their respective labs. Each subject pulled my mind in a different direction, and learning to switch between programming logic, mathematical proofs, and physics concepts in the same week was a skill in itself.</p>

<p>Programming Fundamentals turned out to be one of the subjects I enjoyed the most. It was taught by Dr. Bilal Ahmad, who is an AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning expert, and he brought that same mindset into how he taught us Python from the very first lecture. Instead of just teaching syntax, he taught us to think like programmers who would eventually build real, data-driven systems. Writing my first working Python program and watching it actually execute correctly was one of those small but unforgettable moments of the semester. Building on that foundation, Dr. Bilal also assigned us a Machine Learning project focused on prediction, which gave us a hands-on introduction to how the models he works with in his own research are actually built and trained. For first-semester students, having a real prediction-based ML project felt ambitious, but it pushed us to learn far more than a regular assignment would have.</p>

<p>Calculus and Discrete Mathematics, on the other hand, tested my patience in a completely different way. Numbers and proofs don't forgive shortcuts, and I had to relearn how to actually sit down and work through a problem step by step instead of rushing to an answer.</p>

<p>The last week before exams was its own story. Quizzes, assignments, and deadlines from almost every subject collapsed into the same seven days, mostly because I hadn't spread my preparation out earlier in the semester. Mornings were rushed, evenings were spent buried in notes, and there was barely time to eat properly. But that week also taught me one of the clearest lessons of the semester: cramming everything into the final days is not a strategy, it's a risk — and next semester, I want to revise consistently instead of waiting for panic to push me into it.</p>

<p>Despite the chaos, this semester also gave me one of my proudest moments — actually completing that Machine Learning prediction project in my very first semester. It wasn't perfect, but finishing something that felt completely out of reach just months earlier gave me real confidence going forward.</p>

<p>Looking back at these first few months, I realize that this semester wasn't just about subjects and grades — it was about learning to live independently, manage my own time, and push through moments where giving up would have been easier. I'm carrying all of that into Semester 2.</p>

</div>
